Ellen Persson

I am a 3rd year Ph.D. Candidate in Mathematics at New York University's Courant Institute, under the co-supervision of Georg Stadler and Jonathan Weare. My research is broadly focused on rare event simulation in physical systems, with applications ranging from glaciology to turbulence and fluid models.

Education

  • Ph.D. in Mathematics, New York University (2023 - Present)
  • B.S. in Engineering Mathematics and Statistics, UC Berkeley (2022)

Publications

  • Szymanski, N.J., Fu, S., Persson, E. et al. Integrated analysis of X-ray diffraction patterns and pair distribution functions for machine-learned phase identification. npj Comput Mater 10, 45 (2024).

Research & Projects

  • Temporal Difference Methods for Fluid Dynamics: I am currently working on developing a framework which integrates neural networks with temporal difference methods to compute statistical properties of physical systems. I also contribute source code to GeophysicalFlows.jl, an open-source Julia library for fluid modeling.
  • Computational Methods for Rare Events: I also study tipping point behavior in glaciology models using adjoint-based automatic differentiation to quantify the sensitivity of ice sheet retreat to unknown parameters.

Undergraduate Research

  • Congressional Graph Theory: Under the supervision of Professor Tony Harkin at RIT, I designed a graph theoretical framework using Python, R, and SQL to study the US Congress and presented research results at the 2022 Joint Math Meetings.
  • NLP in Flaubert’s Madame Bovary: Through the UC Berkeley Data Science Discovery Program, I studied governmental censorship in 19th-century French literature using logistic regression, natural language processing, and neural networks.
