Ellen Persson

I am a 3rd year Ph.D. Candidate in Mathematics at New York University's Courant Institute, under the co-supervision of Georg Stadler and Jonathan Weare. My research is broadly focused on rare event simulation in physical systems, with applications ranging from glaciology to turbulence and fluid models.

Education

  • Ph.D. in Mathematics, New York University (2023 - Present)
  • B.S. in Engineering Mathematics and Statistics, UC Berkeley (2022)

Publications

  • Szymanski, N.J., Fu, S., Persson, E. et al. Integrated analysis of X-ray diffraction patterns and pair distribution functions for machine-learned phase identification. npj Comput Mater 10, 45 (2024).

Research & Projects

  • Temporal Difference Methods for Fluid Dynamics: Developing a framework integrating neural networks and automatic differentiation with temporal difference methods to compute statistical properties of physical systems.
  • Computational Methods for Rare Events: Investigating tipping point behavior in glaciology models using adjoint-based automatic differentiation to quantify the sensitivity of ice sheet retreat to unknown basal friction coefficients.

Additional projects and code can be found on my GitHub.
